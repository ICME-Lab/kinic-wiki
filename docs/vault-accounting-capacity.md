# Dedicated Vault accounting and capacity

Production funding uses the pinned KINIC ledger and user-requested authoritative reconciliation. Default builds reject foreign ledger configuration before transfer dispatch. Existing journals, holds, balances and valid late callback settlement remain intact. Only an explicitly nondeployable synthetic-funding build permits purchases against synthetic ledgers.

Vault stores enforce 200 MiB of charged usage per owner, three Vaults per owner, and 100 MiB of current plaintext per Vault. Schema 14 removes the shared 200 MiB logical quota, the Vault-specific 4 GiB SQLite page ceiling, and shared row admission limits for content and journals. Logical and physical capacity differ. The owner maximum is a quota, and the available stable memory and cycles determine how many owners can reach it.

`get_quota` reports owner and Vault storage. `global_capacity` reports aggregate logical charges and the current accounting reservation. The legacy fields retain their Candid types. `limit` returns `u64::MAX`, `overcommitted` returns `false`, and `overage` returns `0`. These values indicate that Vault admission has no shared byte quota. SQLite page and index overhead, stable-memory VFS metadata, and Wasm heap usage are outside logical accounting.

Whole-row guards bound payments and fee leases to 4,096 bytes, account and reservation rows to 512 bytes, and key-flight rows to 256 bytes. Seven 8 KiB global setting slots are retained. Up to eight fee lookups and eight reconciliation scans may run concurrently. Each wallet can hold 16 funding or Wiki bridge records. Vault retains the separate initial grant and key subsidy eligibility guards and the 10,000-entry audit retention policy.

The authenticated caller owns one prepaid account. Cycle reservations store this owner once; refunds and execution checkpoints settle against the same account even after new work is disabled. Payment wallets and beneficiaries remain separate identities.

Ordinary mutations, including DeleteObject, replay exclusively through `operations`. Its named admission classes are Ordinary, Cleanup, ResourceCleanup and ProtectedKey; one policy controls class counts, result limits and usage charges. `upload_outcomes` separately retains tagged Committed revisions or Cancelled Vault/job pairs under upload IDs and their original begin digest. These lifetimes differ from mutation IDs. Expired pending uploads report Expired until bounded GC produces the same Cancelled outcome as explicit cancellation.

An admitted upload reserves ordinary receipt slots/bytes for each distinct chunk and commit. Pending operations reserve 128 KiB and release unused room when their encoded result is committed. Exact operation retries reuse the same receipt; a fresh-ID duplicate chunk consumes ordinary capacity instead of another upload's reserved completion room. Admitted chunks and commit can use their reservations at the owner quota. New work still requires execution credit.

Cleanup of admitted uploads, documents and Vaults uses resource-specific 512-byte completion credits. Resource-backed cleanup receipts are charged to previously admitted resources, separately from the 512-row owner limit for unreserved cleanup receipts. Cancellation preserves its job across batches; each batch removes at most one ciphertext chunk. Purge immediately fences all object history; collection reclaims retired rows in bounded batches. Zero-credit/full-capacity cleanup remains possible. No-work Collect returns NotFound without creating a receipt, and exact retries replay the original result. Receipt retention is 24 hours.

Fresh key acquisition first uses ordinary operation admission. If content capacity or ordinary receipt slots are exhausted, it can use up to 16 retained protected operations per owner, each bounded to 2,048 bytes with at most 1,024 encoded result bytes. The charge fits within the owner quota. Both admission modes share the existing 100/day owner and 1,000/day global key limits, one/eight in-flight bounds, cycle budget and positive-credit requirements. Exact retries retain their stored class, digest and result for the same 24 hours; changed requests conflict. Exhausting the emergency pool refuses new key operations until capacity is reclaimed. This provides bounded fresh access at full content capacity, not unlimited full-capacity reopens.

Each key-flight row carries the 16-byte target Vault, token, phase and generation. Vault charges the owner 4,096 bytes for this row and retains at most 512 per owner. Indexed collection removes up to 128 eligible settled key rows per batch and preserves live or cancelled-but-unsettled flights. The timer retains its round-robin owner cursor across upgrades.

Before dispatch, Vault allocates the beneficiary account and reserves 1 MiB of SQLite pages for each ICRC payment. A callback or authoritative reconciliation releases the reserve inside the transaction that writes the receipt and credit. A definitive rejection or recovery of a known-unsent quote also releases it. Unknown transfers keep their reserve. The reserve covers expected SQLite page growth during settlement. Canister cycles and stable-memory metadata require separate headroom. Wiki bridge transfers still write their credit and receipt before accepting attached cycles.

Schema 14 validates and migrates the complete schema 13 layout and supported historical layouts in place. Migration installs indexes, removes shared admission guards, and allocates completion reserves for existing unsettled payments. Unsupported or incomplete stores are refused unchanged. See [operations](vault/operations.md) and [validation](vault/validation.md).

Unknown payments beyond 23 hours or 100 attempts remain durable and must use reconciliation rather than another transfer. Use the manual authoritative ledger/archive reconciliation path after those bounds. The wallet could have been debited without beneficiary credit. Retaining a journal, screenshot or controller assertion does not recover funds. Do not automatically issue a replacement transfer or manually create compensating credit. Missing or unavailable evidence keeps the payment pending without creating a replacement transfer.

Payment rows retain a preparing marker and bounded reconciliation state. Native maximum-width tests cover cursor and lease values and terminal states. Known-unsent preparation can safely retry.

Manual reconciliation reads actual ledger/ledger-nominated archive blocks, 100 per update, and never dispatches a transfer. Tuple mismatch, unsupported block types, gaps and unavailable evidence do not authorize credit. A payment already confirmed by a callback or scan is replayed without another balance change. Same-format upgrades preserve reconciliation state.

Storage fees retain the exact fraction: `numerator = prior_remainder + bytes * elapsed_ms * 127000`, with denominator `1073741824 * 1000`. The quotient is charged and the remainder survives storage-size changes and compatible upgrades. Old usage settles before its size changes; checkpoints never move backwards. Exhausted balances discard unpaid whole and fractional fees without negative credit or debt. Each owner settles inside a savepoint in the bounded timer sweep; an owner failure preserves its checkpoint and remainder while healthy owners and the existing cursor progress. Database/savepoint/cursor failures abort the outer transaction and the normal timer retries.

Vault IDs come from the existing persistent operation serial. Completed bounded Vault deletion removes the Vault row, so there is no accumulating Vault tombstone or ID-reuse risk. The three-Vault owner bound includes Pending and Deleting rows. Financial journals and operation receipts retain their independent bounded lifetimes.
